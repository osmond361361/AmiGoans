Goan Cuisine

Rice, seafood, coconut and spice — the dishes at the heart of Goan food, with recipes and videos to try at home.

Goan cuisine consists of regional foods popular in Goa, India. Rice, seafood, coconut, vegetables, meat, bread, pork and local spices are staples of Goan cuisine. Use of kokum and vinegar is another distinct feature, and Goan food is considered incomplete without fish. Centuries of Portuguese influence also shaped a unique Indo-Portuguese repertoire found nowhere else in India.

Popular Dishes & Recipes

Vindaloo

A fiery Goan curry of Portuguese origin, traditionally pork marinated in vinegar and garlic and cooked in a spicy chilli paste — tangier and more complex than the British curry-house version it inspired.

Ingredients

  • 750g pork shoulder, cubed
  • 8–10 dried Kashmiri chillies
  • 6 cloves garlic, 1in ginger
  • 1 tsp each cumin, peppercorns, mustard seeds
  • Cinnamon, 4 cloves, ½ tsp turmeric
  • 4 tbsp vinegar, 2 onions, oil, salt

Method

  1. Soak chillies, blend with spices, garlic, ginger and vinegar into a paste.
  2. Marinate the pork for 4+ hours, ideally overnight.
  3. Fry onions until golden, add pork and brown.
  4. Add water, cover and simmer 45–60 min until tender.
  5. Season and serve with rice or poee bread.

Xacuti

A curry with complex spicing — white poppy seeds, toasted coconut and dried red chillies — traditionally made with chicken, lamb, beef or crab.

Ingredients

  • 1 chicken, jointed (~750g)
  • 1 cup grated coconut
  • 2 tbsp white poppy seeds
  • 8 dried red chillies
  • Coriander & cumin seeds, cloves, cinnamon, cardamom, star anise
  • 2 onions, garlic, ginger, oil, salt

Method

  1. Dry-roast coconut and whole spices, then grind to a paste.
  2. Fry onions until golden, add garlic and ginger.
  3. Add the spice paste, cook until oil separates.
  4. Add chicken and water, simmer 30–35 min until cooked.
  5. Season and serve with rice.

Sorpotel

A spicy, tangy pork dish of Portuguese origin, now a Goan Christmas and celebration staple, cooked in a thick vinegar-based sauce.

Ingredients

  • 500g pork (shoulder or belly)
  • 8 dried red chillies
  • Cumin, peppercorns, cinnamon
  • 6 cloves garlic, 1in ginger, ½ tsp turmeric
  • 5 tbsp vinegar, 2 onions, green chillies

Method

  1. Boil pork until just cooked, dice, reserve the liquid.
  2. Grind chillies, spices, garlic, ginger and vinegar to a paste.
  3. Fry onions, add paste and cook until fragrant.
  4. Add pork and reserved liquid, simmer 30 min until thick.
  5. Rest overnight — sorpotel is said to taste best the next day.

Bebinca

A rich, layered dessert sometimes called the "Queen of Goan Desserts", traditionally baked in up to sixteen individual layers and especially popular at Christmas.

Ingredients

  • 10 egg yolks
  • 400ml thick coconut milk
  • 250g sugar
  • 150g plain flour
  • 100g ghee, melted
  • ¼ tsp grated nutmeg

Method

  1. Whisk yolks and sugar, mix in coconut milk, flour and nutmeg.
  2. Grease a dish with ghee, pour a thin (~5mm) layer of batter.
  3. Bake/grill until set and golden, brush with ghee.
  4. Pour and bake each next layer on top; repeat 7–16 times.
  5. Cool fully before turning out and slicing.

Balchão

A bright, tangy pickle-curry of prawns (or other seafood) cooked in a spicy vinegar sauce — often made in a big batch and kept as a store-cupboard staple.

Choris (Goan Sausage)

A spiced, vinegar-cured pork sausage descended from Portuguese chouriço, adapted with local chillies and preserved by pickling the meat before curing. Pan-fried and stuffed into a bread roll, it's the classic choris pao (sausage pao) — or served simply with rice.

Feni

Goa's signature spirit, double-distilled from fermented cashew (or coconut) fruit juice. The single-distilled, lighter version is called urrak.

Cafreal

A vivid green, herb-and-chilli marinated poultry dish brought to Goan cuisine by the Portuguese and the African soldiers who served under them — usually chicken cafreal, grilled or pan-fried whole or in pieces.

Fish Thali

The everyday Goan meal: rice, fish curry (kodi), fried fish, kismur (a dried prawn and coconut salad), pickle and sol kadi, all served together on one platter.

Pão / Poee

Goa's own bread culture: poee (pão) is a soft, pocketed leavened bread baked by traditional paders. The same bread rolls are the base for two beloved street snacks — cutlet pao (a crumbed, fried meat cutlet in a roll) and choris pao — sold at stalls and bakeries across Goa.
